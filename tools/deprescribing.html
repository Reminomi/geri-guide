Deprescribing framework

StepDetail
1 · Establish goalsClarify life expectancy, goals of care, and what the patient values — this determines whether long-term preventive medications remain appropriate.
2 · Review every medicationConfirm current indication still applies, benefit still plausible in remaining life expectancy, and no significant harm/interaction/burden outweighs it.
3 · PrioritiseTarget medications with the highest harm-to-benefit ratio first: anticholinergic/sedative burden, falls-risk drugs, drugs without a clear ongoing indication, drugs treating side effects of other drugs (prescribing cascades).
4 · Sequence the taperOne class at a time where feasible. Taper (don't abruptly stop) benzodiazepines, opioids, antidepressants, beta-blockers, corticosteroids and antiepileptics; most other classes can usually be stopped directly.
5 · MonitorExplicit monitoring plan for withdrawal effects and disease recurrence; document a review date.
6 · Document & communicateRecord shared decision-making and rationale; communicate changes to GP/pharmacist and update any dose-administration aid.

Beers-type high-risk classes — overview

Complementary to STOPP/START; used alongside it, coverage differs. Broad high-risk categories to check on any medication list: benzodiazepines and non-benzodiazepine sedative-hypnotics ("Z-drugs"), strong anticholinergics (first-generation antihistamines, some bladder antimuscarinics, tricyclic antidepressants), long-acting sulfonylureas, NSAIDs (chronic use), skeletal muscle relaxants, antipsychotics for BPSD outside a time-limited, monitored trial, and digoxin at doses above 0.125 mg/day.

Limited life expectancy / comfort-focused goals

Prioritise STOPP over START. Deprescribe medications whose benefit accrues only over years (statins for primary prevention, bisphosphonates, tight glycaemic control, most preventive screening-adjacent medications) while retaining or adding medications for symptom control. Document the goals-of-care conversation that supports this shift.
