Neurocognitive Disorder — Diagnostic Algorithm

1

Presentation

Cognitive, functional or behavioural concern in an older adult — self-report, informant report, incidental finding, or high-risk screening.

1

History + mandatory collateral history

  • Onset/course · domain(s) affected · functional trajectory (IADL before ADL)
  • Medications, alcohol/substances, mood/psychiatric history, sleep (RBD/OSA)
  • Vascular risk, head injury, family history (age of onset), occupational/toxin/HIV risk, malignancy
  • Sensory function, cultural/linguistic/education background

Delirium screen — 4AT / CAM positive?

4AT (0–12): alertness · AMT4 (age/DOB/place/year) · attention (months backwards) · acute/fluctuating change over 2 weeks. ≥4 = probable delirium; 1–3 = possible cognitive impairment, delirium not excluded; 0 = delirium unlikely (doesn't fully exclude it if alertness/attention couldn't be properly tested). CAM: positive requires Feature 1 (acute onset & fluctuating course) AND Feature 2 (inattention), plus either Feature 3 (disorganised thinking) or Feature 4 (altered consciousness).

Yes — delirium presentTreat the precipitant. Do not finalise NCD severity/aetiology (Criterion C). Reassess cognition after resolution (weeks).
No / resolvedProceed to basic assessment →
2

Basic necessary assessment — complete for every patient

  • Cognitive: MoCA (general) / RUDAS (CALD, low literacy) / KICA (Indigenous Australians) / MMSE (if PBS medication pathway relevant) + IQCODE from informant if direct testing unreliable
  • Functional: IADL (Lawton) + ADL (Katz/Barthel)
  • Behavioural/mood: NPI-Q, GDS-15 (or Cornell if NCD established)
  • Caregiver: Zarit Burden Interview
  • Exam: gait, parkinsonism, focal signs, eye movements, myoclonus, orthostatics
  • Bloods: FBC, EUC, Ca, glucose, LFT, TFT, B12/folate (± syphilis/HIV if risk factors)
  • Imaging: MRI brain (preferred) or CT

Run in parallel — bedside "walk around the brain" & atypical screen: probe each cognitive domain for its earliest symptom (behaviour, language, semantics, praxis, visuospatial, perception, face recognition, executive, memory) — do not anchor on a memory-led pattern by default.

3

Apply DSM-5-TR criteria → Major or Mild NCD

CriterionMajor NCDMild NCD
ASignificant decline in ≥1 domain, based on concern (patient/informant/clinician) + substantial impairment on testingModest decline in ≥1 domain, same two evidence sources, milder degree
BInterferes with independence (≥ assistance with IADLs)Independence preserved, though greater effort/compensation may be needed
CNot exclusively during delirium
DNot better explained by another mental disorder

Specifiers: aetiological subtype · behavioural disturbance (with/without; Major NCD — specify agitation/anxiety/mood/psychotic/other) · severity (Major only: mild = IADL difficulty, moderate = basic ADL difficulty, severe = fully dependent).

5

Any atypical / red-flag feature present?

Yes — atypicalYoung onset · rapid course · early behaviour/language change · psychiatric prodrome · focal signs → trigger extended investigations, apply relevant consensus criteria (McKeith / Rascovsky / Gorno-Tempini / Graus / WHO-CJD), consider urgent/specialist referral. See full red-flag table below.
No — typicalProceed with standard aetiological subtyping (NIA-AA/IWG-2 for AD, VASCOG for vascular) using the basic work-up already completed.
4

Assign aetiology (or "multiple" / "unspecified")

Alzheimer disease · Vascular · Lewy body · FTD (behavioural / language) · Parkinson disease dementia · TBI-related · Substance/medication-induced · HIV · Prion · Huntington · another medical condition · multiple aetiologies · unspecified. See the full aetiological subtyping table.

7

Before finalising — exclude mimics

Functional cognitive disorder · Depression ("pseudodementia") · Delirium (acute or superimposed) · Medication/polypharmacy effect · Alcohol/substance use · Metabolic/endocrine cause (thyroid, B12, renal, hepatic, calcium, glucose) · Uncorrected hearing/vision impairment · Obstructive sleep apnoea · Normal pressure hydrocephalus · Structural lesion (tumour, subdural, chronic infection)

8

Formulation

[Major/Mild] NCD due to [aetiology], [with/without] behavioural disturbance, [severity]. Supported by: cognitive scores · functional assessment · collateral history · imaging · extended investigation results (if performed). Atypical features considered and mimics excluded, stated explicitly.

9

Management, safety & referral

Disclosure · driving/capacity/advance care planning · caregiver support · PBS-eligible pharmacotherapy where appropriate · BPSD management · specialist referral (memory clinic / neurology / neuropsychiatry / genetics / prion unit) · follow-up interval matched to trajectory

What ultimately separates MCI from dementia is functional independence, not the test score (Criterion B). Formal neuropsychology (reference standard): ≈1–1.5 SD below norms in ≥1 domain with preserved function → MCI; >1.5–2 SD, often multi-domain, with functional dependency → dementia. NPI-Q, GDS-15, Zarit, 4AT and CAM have no MCI-vs-dementia threshold — they characterise behaviour, mood, caregiver strain and delirium independently.

Mimics & reversible contributors to exclude

Functional cognitive disorder · Depression ("pseudodementia") · Delirium (acute or superimposed) · Medication/polypharmacy effect · Alcohol/substance use · Metabolic/endocrine cause (thyroid, B12, renal, hepatic, calcium, glucose) · Uncorrected hearing/vision impairment · Obstructive sleep apnoea · Normal pressure hydrocephalus · Structural lesion (tumour, subdural, chronic infection)

Functional cognitive disorder clues: internal inconsistency (fine unless attention drawn to the task) · detailed/over-inclusive account of lapses · patient more concerned than informant, often attends alone · disastrous test scores despite intact daily function · obsessional traits. Contrast with AD (face-saving/minimisation) or reduced-insight indifference. Still keep under review — can herald neurodegeneration in older patients.
