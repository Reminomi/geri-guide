Frailty Assessment

Clinical Frailty Scale for rapid bedside/clinic judgement, plus the Fried phenotype for a criterion-based cross-check.

Important: Frailty scales support clinical reasoning; they do not replace it. Use in conjunction with the full history, function, and cognition assessed elsewhere in the Clinic Companion. Neither scale should be applied during an acute deterioration as if it reflects baseline.

Why frailty status changes the plan

Frailty levelPractical implication
Fit / managing well (CFS 1–3, robust)Standard disease-focused management; opportunistic prevention (falls, bone health, vaccination) still applies.
Vulnerable / mild-moderate (CFS 4–6, pre-frail/frail)Comprehensive geriatric assessment indicated; anticipate reduced physiological reserve for surgery/acute illness; proactive falls, medication, nutrition and function review; consider goals-of-care conversation.
Severe–very severe (CFS 7–8)High risk with any acute stressor (surgery, hospitalisation, new medication); prioritise comfort and function over disease-modifying intensity where goals support this; carer/placement needs likely; ACP central to planning.
Terminally ill (CFS 9)Palliative-focused care planning regardless of frailty trajectory; not itself a frailty severity marker.
