Delirium Screening — 4AT

Rapid delirium/cognitive-impairment screen. Takes under 2 minutes; no special training required.

Important: The 4AT is a screening tool, not a diagnostic instrument. A positive screen requires clinical assessment for delirium (precipitant identification, DSM-5-TR criteria) and does not itself confirm the diagnosis. Score all four items even if the patient is drowsy or uncooperative — an inability to engage is scored, not left blank.

1 · Alertness

Includes patients who are agitated/hyperactive as well as those who are drowsy/sleepy. Observe the patient. If asleep, gently attempt to wake by voice or touch; ask them to state their name and address to assist rating.

2 · AMT4

Age, date of birth, place (name of the hospital/building), current year.

3 · Attention

Ask: "Please tell me the months of the year in backwards order, starting at December." To assist initial understanding, one prompt of "what is the month before December?" is permitted.

4 · Acute change or fluctuating course

Evidence of significant change or fluctuation in alertness, cognition, or other mental function (e.g. paranoia, hallucinations) arising over the last 2 weeks and still evident in the last 24 hours, from collateral history and/or observation.

Result

Complete all four items above.

Interpretation reference

Total scoreInterpretation
4 or abovePossible delirium ± cognitive impairment. Assess further for delirium (identify and treat precipitant; do not stage cognitive/NCD status until the acute episode has resolved).
1–3Possible cognitive impairment. Delirium is less likely on this screen alone but is not excluded — interpret alongside clinical context and collateral history. Arrange formal cognitive assessment once medically stable.
0Delirium or severe cognitive impairment unlikely — but does not rule out either, especially if collateral history for item 4 was limited or unavailable.

Next steps if delirium is possible

DomainAction
Precipitant screenInfection, medication (new/changed, anticholinergic/sedative burden), metabolic/electrolyte disturbance, urinary retention, constipation/faecal loading, pain, hypoxia, alcohol/substance withdrawal, environment change, sensory deprivation (glasses/hearing aids not in use).
ExaminationVitals, hydration status, focal neurology, signs of infection, bladder/bowel review.
Investigations (guided by history/exam)FBC, EUC, calcium, glucose, LFTs, TFTs, CRP, urinalysis/MSU, chest X-ray, ECG; further work-up (CT brain, cultures) only if indicated.
ManagementTreat identified precipitant(s); non-pharmacological delirium bundle (orientation, reduce noise/overstimulation, consistent staff, mobilise, correct sensory deficits, sleep-wake cycle support, family/carer presence); avoid physical restraint; medication only for severe distress/safety risk unresponsive to other measures, lowest dose, shortest duration, regular review.
DocumentationNote precipitating/predisposing factors, plan for repeat cognitive assessment after resolution, and communicate delirium episode to GP/next care team — it is a marker of future dementia and mortality risk.

4AT (4 A's Test) — widely used, validated delirium screening instrument; no license required, no special training needed to administer. This page reproduces the scoring structure and standard interpretation bands for bedside use; confirm against the current published 4AT instrument for training and audit purposes. Not a substitute for clinical judgement or formal DSM-5-TR delirium assessment. No patient information entered here is stored or transmitted.
