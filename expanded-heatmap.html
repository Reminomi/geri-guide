The matrix now includes Alzheimer’s disease, vascular dementia, Lewy body dementia, behavioural variant FTD, primary progressive aphasia, posterior cortical atrophy, Parkinson disease dementia, Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease, HIV-associated dementia, dialysis dementia, and normal-pressure hydrocephalus.
Clinical anchors used for expansion include recognised PPA variants and their language-led presentation, PCA as a visual-predominant posterior syndrome, CJD as a rapidly progressive dementia with myoclonus and ataxia, and HIV-associated dementia as a subcortical slowing-gait syndrome.

Pattern summary

  • PPA is treated as a language-led umbrella syndrome, with early isolated aphasia and relative sparing of other domains in the initial phase.
  • PCA is weighted toward higher-order visual dysfunction and visuospatial breakdown with relative early sparing of memory and behaviour.
  • CJD is represented as a rapidly progressive syndrome with prominent myoclonus, cerebellar or motor findings, and severe subacute decline.
  • HIV-associated dementia is framed as a subcortical syndrome marked by psychomotor slowing, gait-motor change, and executive impairment.
  • Dialysis dementia is included as a historical metabolic-toxic syndrome with dysarthria, myoclonus, seizures, and rapid progression.

Use notes

  • This figure is best used for teaching syndrome recognition and differential framing rather than definitive diagnosis.
  • Some labels are umbrella terms. PPA contains nonfluent/agrammatic, semantic, and logopenic variants, each with distinct profiles.
  • The next precision upgrade would split PPA into nfvPPA, svPPA, and lvPPA, and potentially split PCA from atypical Alzheimer disease variants.
