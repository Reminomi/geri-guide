Cholinesterase inhibitors (ChEI)

First-line symptomatic therapy for mild-to-moderate Alzheimer disease; also used in Parkinson disease dementia and, cautiously, DLB. Modest effect on cognition/function; does not alter underlying trajectory.

Common side effects (class): nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, anorexia, weight loss, vivid dreams/insomnia, muscle cramps, bradycardia.

Cautions/contraindications: sick sinus syndrome or other conduction disease without a pacemaker, concurrent bradycardic agents, active peptic ulcer disease, asthma/COPD (bronchospasm risk), bladder outflow obstruction, seizure disorder.

Switching within class: reasonable if intolerant of one agent rather than abandoning the class; allow washout/direct switch per product information.

Memantine

NMDA-receptor antagonist. Used in moderate-to-severe Alzheimer disease, alone or added to a ChEI; also used in some non-Alzheimer dementias where agitation is prominent.

5 mg dailyIncrease by 5 mg/week20 mg daily (often as 10 mg BD, or once-daily formulation)Reduce target dose in significant renal impairment (confirm against product information for eGFR-specific dosing)

Common side effects: dizziness, headache, constipation — generally well tolerated. Caution: concurrent NMDA-antagonist/related agents; limited evidence with concurrent uncontrolled seizure disorder.

Eligibility & response review — general structure

Subsidised-prescribing schemes (e.g. Australia's PBS) commonly structure ChEI/memantine access around a diagnosis confirmed with specialist input, an objective baseline severity measure, and a mandated response review — but exact score bands and Authority codes are updated periodically.

Typical structureDetail
DiagnosisAlzheimer disease diagnosis, usually with specialist (geriatrician/psychogeriatrician/neurologist) involvement documented
Baseline severityObjective score at initiation, commonly framed around a mild-to-moderate MMSE band for ChEI initiation, and a lower band for memantine — confirm current cut-offs before prescribing
Response reviewReassessment at approximately 6 months using the same or a comparable instrument; continuation typically requires evidence of stabilisation or improvement, not just continued use
Non-Alzheimer useOff-label/unsubsidised use in DLB, PDD or vascular-predominant NCD is common in practice and often reasonable, but funding criteria typically do not cover it — confirm and document accordingly

Confirm current criteria on the live PBS (or local) schedule at the time of prescribing — these bands change.

BPSD pharmacotherapy

Black-box-level warning: antipsychotics in older people with dementia-related psychosis/agitation are associated with increased risk of stroke and death. Use only after non-pharmacological measures and reversible-cause review have failed, at the lowest effective dose, for the shortest duration, with regular documented attempts to reduce/cease. Avoid in DLB/PDD outside exceptional, closely monitored circumstances — risk of severe/life-threatening neuroleptic sensitivity reactions.

Sequence:

  1. Identify and treat reversible precipitants first — pain, constipation, infection, urinary retention, sensory deficit, overstimulation/understimulation, unmet need, delirium.
  2. Non-pharmacological strategies — behavioural analysis (ABC charting), environmental modification, structured activity, caregiver education/support, music/reminiscence approaches.
  3. Pharmacotherapy only if there is risk to the patient or others, or severe distress unresponsive to the above.

Stopping / deprescribing ChEI and memantine

See also the Deprescribing toolkit for the general framework. Specific triggers for these agents:

  • Advanced/severe dementia with no evident ongoing benefit
  • Adverse effects outweighing benefit (bradyarrhythmia, GI intolerance, significant renal impairment complicating memantine dosing)
  • Swallowing difficulty or administration burden disproportionate to benefit
  • Patient/family preference, or a shift to comfort-focused goals of care

Pharmacological taper is not usually mandatory, but withdraw one agent at a time with monitoring — evidence for post-cessation behavioural worsening is limited but described; discuss this possibility, and the option of restarting, with the patient/family in advance.
